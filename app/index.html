What you are seeing

Glaciers flow downhill like very slow rivers of ice. Satellites observe the same place days to weeks apart; by matching surface features between the two observations we measure how far — and in which direction — the ice moved. Map colours are that horizontal speed, in metres per year (m/yr).

How to use

  1. Pick a map layer on the left — the box under it explains the colours.
  2. Click the glacier to see its speed over time (Esc or “Clear pin” removes the pin).
  3. Analysis → Along the glacier: hover the chart; a yellow dot follows on the map.
  4. Tick Show 3-D relief to see the terrain; Reset view (top-right) returns to the start.

How displacement was converted into velocity

  1. GOFF gives two offsets per pixel: along-track (direction of satellite flight) and slant-range (change in distance to the satellite), in metres.
  2. A constant bias is removed from each pair using stable rock (ITS_LIVE speed ≤ 3 m/yr), which should not move.
  3. Offsets are projected to ground motion with the product’s own radar geometry (line-of-sight and along-track unit vectors from its radarGrid).
  4. Where only one viewing direction exists, east/north motion is solved assuming flow parallel to the surface (vertical = 0). Where ascending and descending pairs overlap, east, north and up are solved by least squares with no such assumption.
  5. Displacement ÷ time interval × 365.25 = velocity in m/yr. Pairs from the same season and direction are combined by per-pixel median.

The inversion is checked by an automated test that recovers a known synthetic velocity field exactly.

Quality filtering and uncertainty

  • NISAR: removed pixels flagged invalid or water in the product mask; signal-to-noise < 5; offsets larger than 30 m; values differing from their 5×5 neighbourhood median by > 2 m; speeds > 800 m/yr (impossible for Siachen). Uncertainty per pair = spread of motion on stable rock (table above).
  • ITS_LIVE: kept a pixel-year only if ≥ 5 image pairs contributed, the error was ≤ 20 m/yr or ≤ 25 % of the speed, and speed < 1,500 m/yr. Errors are shown as the shaded band / ± values in charts and as the “measurement precision” layer.
  • Glacier ice = median 2017–2022 speed ≥ 15 m/yr; stable rock = ≤ 3 m/yr.

How seasonal change and hotspots were calculated

  • Seasons: winter = Nov–Apr (4 pairs), summer = May–Oct (3 pairs). Summer − winter is computed only where both seasons were measured. Headline: fast ice (≥ 40 m/yr) .
  • Trend: per-pixel least-squares slope of ITS_LIVE speed against year (≥ 4 valid years), with its standard error.
  • Significant change: the trend is ≥ 2 standard errors from zero and the 2017→2022 change exceeds 2 × its combined measurement error, in the same direction.
  • Hotspots: connected (8-neighbour) areas of significant change ≥ 0.25 km², ranked by area.
  • Centreline: least-cost path through the fastest ice from the upper basin to the snout (35.205° N, 77.192° E).

Known coverage gaps and limitations

  • NISAR (2025–26) and ITS_LIVE (2017–22) do not overlap in time; we compare spatial patterns, not the same period.
  • Summer NISAR covers less ice (upper glacier gaps where wet snow prevents radar matching); single-direction areas assume surface-parallel flow.
  • Vertical ice motion was computed where both directions exist but is too noisy (≈ ±18 m/yr on rock) to report. Thinning or thickening is not measured by this tool; the 3-D relief is a present-day terrain model, exaggerated only for display.
  • Hotspots near the edge of the study area may be clipped; only hotspots inside it are listed.
  • NISAR products are early (Beta / Provisional) releases and may be reprocessed by NASA/JPL.
